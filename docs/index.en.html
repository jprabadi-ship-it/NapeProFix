What it does

No Hammerspoon, no Karabiner. The app is self-contained.

Map all four directions

Assign a built-in action or any keyboard shortcut to each of the up, down, left, and right gestures.

Layers 0–7

Eight sets of assignments. Switch between them from the menu, or with a shortcut you record yourself.

Rotation correction

If an Octa Shift reset leaves the orientation off, rotate it back in 90° steps from the menu. No need to reconfigure the device.

Scrolling that accelerates

Keep rolling and each gesture scrolls further, from the default 8 lines up to 32. Flip the direction if it feels backwards.

Clicks land where you aim

On a trackball the ball turns a little as your finger presses and lifts, so the click lands a few pixels off target. The cursor is held still while the button is down and put back where it started. Dragging still works.

Launch at login

One setting, and the app is there every time you start your Mac.

Setup guide built in

What to configure in Keychron Launcher is documented inside the app, with a diagram. It also appears automatically on first launch.

Getting started

Two things need setting up in Keychron Launcher. The same instructions are always available in the app under "Launcher setup".

1. Keep gesture mode always on

In Advanced mode, turn on "Always enable gesture mode". Without it, gestures only fire while you hold the button assigned to Ball Gesture.

Worth knowing

Why modifier keys must not be used

When Launcher adds a modifier, that modifier is fully pressed and released on every single gesture. Roll continuously and it behaves like Option being hammered, which makes apps misfire.

The app cannot work around this. flagsChanged arrives before the character key, so at that moment there is no way to tell a gesture from ordinary typing, and the device cannot be identified either. The four keys above do not exist on a Mac keyboard and do nothing when pressed on their own, which is what makes them safe to use unmodified.

Pause and Scroll Lock cannot be used

Pause has no key code on macOS and the event disappears entirely. Scroll Lock becomes F14, which the system consumes at a low level as brightness-down on older Mac keyboards, so no app can intercept it.

Choosing a layer-switch key

The app cannot tell which device input came from — the event tap carries no device information. A key you register behaves the same when pressed on your keyboard directly, and that key becomes unavailable to other apps.

So you have to pick a key you are willing to give up system-wide. Numpad keys such as × collide on a Mac with a numeric keypad; ⌃⌥ plus a letter may collide with other apps' shortcuts.

The "modifier pressed on every gesture" problem is specific to gestures, which fire repeatedly as you roll. A button press happens once, so modifiers are fine for layer switching.

Recovering after resetting Launcher

A reset clears the gesture key assignments — and also returns the Octa Shift rotation to its default. Restoring only the keys leaves the orientation off.

Rotation exists on both the device and the app, so changing one alone always leaves them mismatched. After restoring the keys, choose "Reset to 0°" in the app menu, and if it is still off, "Rotate 90°".

Gatekeeper blocks the app on another Mac

The app is not notarized. On macOS 15 and later, right-click → Open no longer works around this. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and press "Open Anyway" near the bottom.
